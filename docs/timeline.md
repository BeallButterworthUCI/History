# A Chronological Narrative of the UCI Beall Butterworth Competition

[← Back to main archive](../README.md)

The per-year files in [years/](../years/) are the authoritative ledger. This document is the storyline that connects them - the readable arc of how a small Donald Bren School software contest became a 200+ team annual showcase that produces venture-backed companies.

## Era 1: The Butterworth Product Development Competition (~2003 - 2013)

The competition's earliest cycles ran inside the Donald Bren School of Information and Computer Sciences as a single-track software event. Paul Butterworth (B.S. CS '74, M.S. CS '81) - by then well into a career that had already taken him through Ingres, Forte Software (acquired by Sun in 1999 for ~$540M), Sun Microsystems, Oracle, and AmberPoint - sponsored the contest as a way to pull a "product mindset" into a curriculum that mostly trained engineers, not founders.

The 2014 Samueli press release calls 2014 the "11th annual" Butterworth, and judge Kevin Kinsey's 2012 quote referenced "nine years" of competitors. Both anchor the inaugural cycle to roughly 2003-2004.

What this era looked like, from the records that survived:

- **Format:** ~10 finalist teams per year, each with a faculty or industry mentor. Final round in Bren Hall. Single-track (software / ICS only).
- **Prize tier:** $5,000 / $3,500 / $1,500. Total purse $10,000 in 2012; likely similar across the era.
- **Themes:** vertical social networks (Portfelo's modeling-industry portfolio platform, 2012), mobile-first utilities (M-Prescription, Approve), early IoT ideas (SmartHome, RFID inventory in Rims), photo / image understanding (Pictorria), and indoor positioning (B.A.T.'s Bluetooth visitor tracking, 2013).
- **Recurring mentors:** Tony Crisp (CRISPx), Channa Samynathan (Syniverse), Ash Kumra (DreamItAlive), Brian Dao (Alpha Sprouts), Don Patterson (UCI Informatics), Ramesh Jain (UCI Bren Chair), Ian Harris (UCI CS), Ray Chan (K5 Venture Partners), Arie Shen.
- **Recurring judge:** Kevin Kinsey of Netreo, who sat on the 2012 panel and continues today.

**What's lost:** the 2010 (7th annual) and 2011 (8th annual) winners are not currently online. The legacy ICS news archive has 500-errored on the relevant article IDs and Google's index does not surface them. See [years/2010.md](../years/2010.md) and [years/2011.md](../years/2011.md). The 2003 - 2009 winners are similarly absent. Anyone with program booklets or alumni knowledge from those years is invited to contribute via [the missing-year issue template](../.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/02-missing-year.md).

## Era 2: The Inaugural Joint Edition (2014)

2014 changed the competition's structural identity. Donald R. Beall and the Beall Family Foundation funded the **Beall Student Design Competition** at the Henry Samueli School of Engineering, and the two competitions ran a shared awards ceremony for the first time. The combined prize pool jumped to $30,000 ($7,500 / $5,000 / $2,500 per placement, per track).

This was the moment when "BBComp" started to mean what it means today: software meets hardware, ICS meets Engineering, code meets atoms. The inaugural Beall winner Spero Diagnostics (sepsis diagnostic targeting <3 hr / <$100) and the inaugural joint Butterworth winner Sparky / Blueberry (Bluetooth-bubble passive networking app) set the tone - a hardware track with serious clinical ambition, a software track playing with what was then cutting-edge BLE.

David Ochi joined as competition director that year and has remained in the role through 2026 - one of the longest tenure-of-leadership stretches in any UCI student competition.

## Era 3: The IoT, BLE, and AR / VR Years (2015 - 2017)

The mid-decade winners were all variations on the same architectural insight: a small device, a smartphone app, a cloud service.

- 2015: Roco's ML photo-sharing one-shot facial recognition (Butterworth 1st), MuTech's HIV diagnostic device (Beall 1st).
- 2016: Tag Team's crowd-GPS Bluetooth tag (Beall 1st) - a near-exact functional ancestor of Apple's AirTag, launched **five years before** Apple shipped AirTag in 2021. Sonder VR's autism-spectrum social-skills VR therapy (Butterworth 1st). CeleriBio's bloodstream-infection diagnostic (Beall 3rd).
- 2017: Krobotech's affordable competitive prosthetic hand (Beall 1st), Memorlane's collaborative photo-sharing app (Butterworth 1st).

Two structural facts about this era worth noting:

1. **Trajectory teams.** Both the 2017 Beall and Butterworth 1st-place winners (Krobotech, Memorlane) had been finalists in 2016. The "almost won last year, came back to win this year" pattern shows up repeatedly through the archive.
2. **Cross-year careers.** Several individuals competed in two or three cycles. Adrian Bahani placed 3rd in 2017 (Syntr Health) and 3rd again in 2019 (HemaDynamics). The duo Ahmad Razavi and Nazanin Ghasemian fielded four finalist teams across 2016 and 2017.

## Era 4: The Higher Stakes (2018 - 2019)

2018 raised the prize tier to $10,000 / $6,500 / $3,500 per placement, the level it has held through 2026. The cycle began to attract larger, more interdisciplinary teams - CrewView's 2018 Butterworth 2nd-place AR / VR collaboration tool listed eight student members, the largest roster in the joint era to that point.

This era also produced the first BBComp team to incorporate as a venture-backed company: **CenSyn Inc.**, founded immediately after the 2019 Beall 2nd-place placement by Ayushi Patel, Andy Bhushan, and Trevor Silence. CenSyn went on to raise a $105K Berkeley SkyDeck seed round in 2021, recruit Peter Crosby (40 years in medical devices) as Executive Chairman, and pursue FDA 510(k) clearance for the CenSyn PenEEG.

The **AllerChain → forMED Technologies** trajectory begins here: the 2019 combined-track finalist team (Lily McGrale, Aryan Agarwal, Ruben Shakya, Raphael Francis Aguas, Kamalesh Ananthakrishnan) returned in 2021 with a new product (TonoLase glaucoma monitor) and won 1st place. This is the clearest "finalist with one product, winner with the next" arc in the archive.

## Era 5: Pandemic Disruption and Recovery (2020 - 2022)

The 2020 cycle was cancelled outright due to COVID-19. The competition pivoted to virtual planning and held a fully virtual 2021 cycle. By 2022 the ceremony was back in person ("back in full swing," per the ICS write-up) with a >$40,000 total purse.

The 2021 cycle is also when **Grasshopperfund** (Butterworth 1st) became one of the first BBComp winners to publicly enter an incubator (UCI Wayfinder) and a national accelerator (Blackstone LaunchPad / Techstars top 30 of 900). Founder Caitlyn Yang founded a sister venture (StartSmart) and is now a Performance Engineer at SpaceX.

The 2022 Butterworth 1st-place winner **Leprendo** (online farmer's marketplace) became the second BBComp team to operate as an active commercial company. By December 2024 it had reported $400K first-year sales and a $250,000 USDA Farmers Market Promotion Program grant, plus a sweep of the 2022 Stella Zhang New Venture Competition.

## Era 6: Brazil and the International Era (2023+)

2023 added an entirely new track. The **Brazilian Collaboration Prize** paired UCI students with students from the Pontifical Catholic University of Rio Grande do Sul (PUCRS), facilitated by PUCRS associate professor and UCI visiting scholar Rafael Prikladnicki. The inaugural cohort: 33 PUCRS students, 40+ UCI students, 17 international teams. The winning team got a travel award for an exchange visit at the partner university.

In 2025 the prize was rebranded to the **International Collaboration Prize**, signaling that the partnership framework is intended to extend beyond Brazil.

The 2023 Butterworth 1st-place team **Eupnea** (AI mobile / web app for tuberculosis diagnosis) followed up with a Microsoft Imagine Cup Epic Challenge American Region win and a Top 3 finish at the Imagine Cup World Championship - the first BBComp team to compete at a global stage.

## Era 7: Deep Tech and Real Companies (2024 - 2025)

2024 was the year BBComp's commercial track record stopped being theoretical:

- **Sentinel** (Butterworth 1st) - the AI dashcam pitched at trucking-fleet drowsiness detection - is now the commercial product **Sentinel by Spotter AI** at sentinel.spotter.ai. CEO/Co-Founder: Peidi Wu; Co-Founder, CTO and President: Gabriel Stonys. The team also won the 20th Stella Zhang New Venture Competition Grand Finale ($20K + $10K), placed 2nd at the C&C Innovative Solutions SoCal Super Pitch, and won the UCI AI Innovation Challenge.
- **NeoMold** (Beall 1st) - 3D-printable corrective ear molds for newborns - reached the Stella Zhang NVC top 10 of 125 teams (top 2 in Life Sciences). Founder Christine Ly is now a PhD student at the USC BioMEMS lab.
- **Two Cube** (Butterworth 2nd) - autonomous LLM-driven bioinformatics for single-cell RNA - won 1st in the Stella Zhang NVC Life Science track ($10K). Co-founder Shushrruth Sai Srinivasan is a UCI CS PhD student with peer-reviewed publications.

In a single year, all three top-tier joint-track winners placed in the Stella Zhang NVC. That's the kind of result-density the early years would not have predicted.

2025 doubled down on AI-native medicine: Florence (Butterworth 1st - AI patient monitoring), AINGELX (Butterworth 2nd - SaaS-investor matchmaking), NasoCoustics (Beall 1st - portable nasometry for cleft-palate speech therapy), VeinGuard (Beall 2nd - cervical collar that doesn't compress jugular veins), REACH (Beall 3rd - ML electrotherapy for stroke survivors). The International Collaboration Prize went to Oslo / Birdhouse by Oslo (contact-free thermal vital-sign monitoring for senior care).

## Era 8: 2026 (In Progress)

The 2026 cycle is in its final week as this archive's last update. Submissions closed May 6, 2026; Demo Day & Awards on **May 15, 2026, 10 AM - 3 PM at The Cove**. The full 2026 cycle calendar with workshop dates and facilitators lives in [years/2026.md](../years/2026.md). The **Stella Zhang NVC Grand Finale + Awards** is six days later on **May 21, 2026** at the Paul Merage School - the typical 5-7 day gap that BBComp-winning teams use to pivot pitch materials for the NVC.

Adjacent macro events shape the cycle's outcome environment:
- **Vena Vitals (2019 Beall 1st Qardiac follow-on)** opened a new R&D lab at Roseman University of Nevada Summerlin in March 2026 (~60 jobs forecast); FDA clearance anticipated within 2026, market launch targeted late 2027.
- **Sentinel (2024 Butterworth 1st)** was publicly launched as Spotter AI's flagship hiring/screening/compliance platform on February 18, 2026, with a March 31, 2026 expansion adding the digital driver application.
- **CARGO Therapeutics (2016 CeleriBio lead Louai Labanieh, co-founder)** completed acquisition by Concentra Biosciences on August 19, 2025 at $4.379/share + CVR (~$170.7M) - the **first documented BBComp-traceable M&A exit**.
- **Stacey Nicholas Dean Magnus Egerstedt departed UCI** to become Provost / EVC at UNC Chapel Hill effective March 2, 2026. Egerstedt is a Swedish-American roboticist with nearly two decades at Georgia Tech (Steve C. Chaddick School Chair, ECE) prior to becoming UCI Samueli Dean in 2021; he built the **Robotarium** swarm-robotics testbed at Georgia Tech and the **Robot Ecology Lab** at UCI. **Prof. Faryar Jabbari** (MAE, Associate Dean for Academic Affairs since Oct 1, 2011 (later promoted to **Senior Associate Dean for Academic Affairs**), 40+ years on UCI faculty; 200+ publications in control theory applied to energy systems and structural control) became interim Dean of the Henry Samueli School of Engineering effective March 1, 2026, leading the school through the 2026 BBComp cycle. A permanent-dean search committee chaired by Distinguished Prof. **Lee Swindlehurst** (IEEE Fellow, UCI EECS Dept Chair, Nicolaos G. and Sue Curtis Alexopoulos Presidential Chair) was announced May 4, 2026.

Twenty-two annual cycles after Paul Butterworth's first sponsorship and twelve after Donald Beall's, the competition has become what it was always meant to be - a place where engineers and computer scientists ship something, in front of judges who ask hard product questions, and occasionally start companies.
